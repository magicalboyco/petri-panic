# Professor Trung's Petri Panic — World & Story Bible

**Status:** Proposed narrative canon, version 1.0. Prepared before scripting dialogue or changing gameplay.  
**Working title:** Professor Trung's Petri Panic  
**Setting:** Pink Laboratories / the Pink Lab  
**Audience:** Welcoming for the all-ages, original-handheld-adventure audience; never childish or condescending to adults.  
**Tone:** Cozy scientific wonder, sly comedy, appealing mystery, growing responsibility, and warm marriage/friendship.  
**Guiding idea:** Great discoveries begin with curiosity. Great scientists care for what they discover.

> One-sentence pitch: A brilliant, spectacularly absent-minded scientist leaves his newly hired assistant in charge of the cutest living experiment on earth, just as his misbehaving microscope begins revealing impossible connections between life and the history of science.

## The promise to the player

You are not Trung's sidekick watching him be brilliant. You are the assistant whose daily attention keeps the Globs alive and whose observations ultimately solve the problem that the professor's enormous intellect alone cannot solve. The player's chosen name is respected and used correctly. The joke is that Trung misplaces the **name card** or his own notebook, not that the game endlessly misnames the player.

Discoveries must come from gameplay. A player should see something surprising, do something useful, and then hear a few lines of story—not read six dialogue screens before touching the game. Storytelling can be skipped, revisited, or read in the Lab Notebook.

## People

### Professor Trung — inventor, scientist, accidental trouble magnet

- Visual: approved expressive illustration, swept-back silver hair, black glasses, warm smile, mustache/goatee, white coat over a blue shirt, maple-leaf lapel pin.
- Greatest strength: He notices patterns nobody else can see and sincerely delights in every living thing he studies.
- Comic flaw: He forgets the weekday, leaves his glasses on his head, writes his own reminder on the wrong side of the paper, and regularly misplaces his clipboard. His scientific results are real. His desk organization is not.
- Emotional flaw: He is so focused on discoveries that he sometimes misses the moment happening in front of him. His arc is learning to value people, teamwork, and the everyday acts of care as much as astonishing results.
- Relationship to player: A generous but distractible mentor. Praises effort and curiosity, explains science with enthusiasm, and credits the player's achievements properly.
- Dialogue voice: Quick tangents, specific scientific excitement, mild self-correction, a joke that is usually on himself. Never dumps a paragraph of jargon.

### Scott — Trung's husband, musician, and the human who knows when the day matters

- Visual: approved cartoon portrait, dark hair with gray at the sides, glasses, warm smile, pink shirt, black vest and tie, phone and black stringed instrument.
- Strength: Emotionally perceptive, funny, supportive, and never intimidated by science he doesn't need to understand.
- Relationship: Their marriage is established, affectionate, and ordinary in the best sense. Scott isn't a nag, a prize, or a late-game revelation. He loves Trung, and Trung loves him.
- Running story thread: Trung has forgotten the date of an important dinner with Scott. Scott calls with good humor and clear expectations; Trung decides to show up. Returning for the finale is a promise kept.
- Dialogue voice: Short, warm, playfully dry. Often translates Trung's breathless science into plain language; sometimes notices things Trung missed.
- The instrument is part of Scott's visual character; don't invent a real-life occupation for him. He enjoys playing music.

### The Intern — the playable protagonist

- Any player-selected name, with default **Intern**. No presumed gender, age, or appearance.
- Starts with a straightforward task: keep two Globs comfortable while Trung takes a very important call.
- Grows into lead observer, Guardian-team organizer, researcher, and eventually **Lead Glob Researcher**, the existing Experiment 21 promotion.
- The player is indispensable because they notice what happens when the lab is treated as a habitat, not merely as a machine.
- The player has no mandatory dialogue-choice tree. Their actions, discoveries, chosen Guardians and notebook entries tell their personal story.

## The Pink Lab and its impossible instrument

Pink Laboratories is a quirky but serious research center. It contains a research bench with the culture dish, a supply shelf, the Lab Notebook, a Guardian nursery/charging area, and a prototype called the **Epoch Lens**.

The Epoch Lens is Professor Trung's invention: a microscope that recreates the scientific conditions and equipment of different eras around a living culture. It is not a literal machine that sends the player into historical events or changes real history. Instead, different research eras are **laboratory environments and experimental lenses** through which the same underlying mystery becomes visible. This keeps the game's five periods and their different backgrounds coherent.

The Lens was built to ask a reasonable question with an impossible tool: *What can a living system teach us if we examine it through the discoveries of different ages?*

When the player starts researching Globs, the Lens unexpectedly detects a repeating DNA signature across its simulations. The signature is both a breakthrough and a source of instability. The more research the player completes, the more unstable energy and contamination the Lens draws into the culture. Each successful experiment collects three DNA fragments that help bring the instrument back into balance and reveal another piece of the pattern.

The stakes are personal and tangible: an unstable Lens could harm the Globs, wreck years of research, and erase an extraordinary discovery. The story should not suddenly become an apocalypse.

**Science-versus-fantasy clarity:** Globs, the Epoch Lens, DNA fragments as pickups, and Guardian abilities are playful science fiction, not claims about real microbiology or genetics. In notebook entries, we can invite curiosity about real microscopes, habitats, DNA, and ecosystems without presenting the game's fantastical mechanics as fact.

## What lives in a Petri dish?

### Globs

Globs are curious, squishy organisms that respond to moisture, energy, and their environment. They have distinct personalities (Shy, Friendly, Energetic) and grow from Baby to Grown to Expert. When healthy, they produce **BioBits**, a shimmering energy byproduct that powers lab equipment and purchases. Healthy Expert Globs can have babies.

**The central relationship is care, not capture or domination.** The Globs are not weapons. They are little companions whose behavior gives the player clues. Their feelings are readable through animation, faces, sound, and the inspect view. Their health is important because caring for them is the game's moral and mechanical center.

### BioBits and DNA fragments

- **BioBits:** A bright, collectible byproduct of healthy cultures; the lab's upgrade currency.
- **DNA fragments:** Three research clues gathered in each Adventure/Hard experiment, forming a usable reading for the Epoch Lens. They are game abstractions; no real DNA is collected by tapping a golden bubble.
- **Water:** The most immediately understandable way to care for a Glob. Gameplay teaches this with one tap, not a lecture.

### Guardians

The Guardians are the lab's friendly protective companions—part bio-inspired invention, part odd little helper. Their exact fictional material differs: some feel like creatures, others like enchanted-looking laboratory gadgets. They support or protect Globs in 24 mechanically distinct ways already present in the game.

Guardians are not captured from the wild. The player discovers and learns to work with them as their research progresses. A growing Guardian team represents trust, specialization, and the support systems that allow science to happen.

Guardian details belong in the **Lab Notebook**, not a wall of text during onboarding. The companion index in [guardian-notebook.md](guardian-notebook.md) maps to the current IDs, names, effects, and unlock levels in the code.

### Intruders and contamination

Not all microbes are bad, and the game shouldn't teach otherwise. The enemies are **particular disruptive agents in this vulnerable culture**, rather than a moral judgment on all microscopic life:

- **Viruses:** Fast-moving intruders that threaten the Globs. A reason to notice early warnings and to protect the dish.
- **Creeping mold:** An environmental imbalance that spreads if ignored; an invitation to use support and cleanup Guardians.
- **Poisoning:** A condition affecting the culture, not a villain. Care and the right equipment can treat it.
- **Boss anomalies:** Very large, unstable patterns formed as the Epoch Lens loses calibration. They look alarming, but the player succeeds by restoring equilibrium, not because the anomalies are irredeemably evil.

Named bosses and their **existing** experiment positions:

| Experiment | Anomaly | Story purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | Prism Queen | First sign the Lens has awakened a pattern larger than a normal culture. |
| 9 | Spore Serpent | Suggests the pattern changes form as conditions change. |

## The mystery: what are the Globs telling us?

The scientific surprise is not that the Globs are secretly monsters. It's that they can **adapt together**. Across very different laboratory conditions, Globs and Guardians form patterns of cooperation. The Epoch Lens sees that pattern as a repeating signal; when the instrument is pushed too hard, the signal becomes distorted and produces intrusive anomalies.

Trung initially thinks there is one brilliant formula that will solve it. The player, through keeping the culture alive, demonstrates the missing variable: a living system isn't simply a set of measurements. Relationships, resilience, and good care are part of the answer.

Final reveal: the three-fragment readings and the player's observational record let Trung recalibrate the Epoch Lens. **Omega Bloom** is the most spectacular symptom of imbalance, not an evil mastermind. The discovery is world-changing within the story because it changes how the Lab understands complex, cooperative life.

Trung's realization: *The most important observation wasn't on my chart. It was what you did for them when nobody was watching.*

### Ending tone

The finale is uplifting, not solemn. Trung is overwhelmed by the discovery; Scott gently reminds him that the intern did most of the actual work. Trung gives the player the title of **Lead Glob Researcher**, says their chosen name correctly, and writes it in ink across the lab's first completed notebook. Scott offers to bring dinner by the lab next time—**after** their reservation, not instead of it.

## The Lab Notebook (our own collection/field-guide feature)

**Working in-world name:** The Pink Lab Notebook  
**Tagline:** *Half scientific masterpiece. Half coffee-stained reminders.*  
**Premise:** Trung left the assistant a notebook that is full of ambitious headings, unfinished sentences, loose scraps, and room for discoveries. Scott has added a few lovingly exasperated sticky notes. The player gradually finishes it.

The notebook is inspired by the **joy of cataloging and discovering**, not by duplicating another game's names, designs, capture rules, or combat structure.

**Proposed tabs:**
1. **Globs** — personality, growth stage, care cues, discoveries, optional favorite-Glob observations.
2. **Guardians** — 24 discoverable entries with illustrated portrait, name, specialty, unlock, simple practical tip and Trung's handwritten observation.
3. **Intruders** — virus, mold, poisoning, and four named anomaly dossiers; reveal after first encounter.
4. **Discoveries** — one concise clue per story milestone, with the three DNA fragments represented as readings.
5. **Messages** — replay of important Trung and Scott calls, with their actual approved portraits.
6. **Experiments** — completed era stamps, personal bests if already available, and the finale certificate.

**Notebook entry states:** Undiscovered silhouette → observed entry (what it does) → field note (a short, useful lesson) once the player actually interacts with it. Never conceal a Guardian's essential gameplay ability behind discovery gates: locked roster options must still communicate unlock requirements, and active Guardians must have readable instructions.

**Notebook entry template:**
- Name, type, illustration, first found/unlock experiment.
- **What it does:** one plain-language mechanical sentence.
- **In the field:** a short, actionable tip tied to real game behavior.
- **Professor's margin note:** one characterful but optional joke or observation.
- Optional Scott sticky note when a chapter warrants it.

**Unlocking philosophy:** A notebook entry celebrates something the player actually met, selected, survived, or discovered. Never require collecting fictitious species that aren't implemented, or force the player to repeat a tutorial to find a page. Progress persists independently of an active experiment and does not reset when the player pauses.

## Voice and writing rules for the upcoming dialogue pass

- Two **short** onboarding pages, then gameplay; don't expand onboarding again.
- A line should usually fit in **one or two compact mobile speech bubbles** (aim roughly 15–30 words per beat). Long notes live in the optional Notebook.
- Teach the actionable part first: **what is this thing? what can I do? why does it matter?**
- Give creatures names and emotions without making them identical to children or pets.
- Humor comes from Trung's misplaced objects, contradictory calendars, grand scientific analogies and Scott's affectionate timing. Never use forgetfulness to humiliate him or the player.
- Scott and Trung love each other openly, naturally, and without making their marriage an issue for the plot.
- Scientific terms have kid-readable explanations; notebook prose can offer a little more depth for adults.
- Do not copy or paraphrase famous Pokémon lines or visuals. The reference is **pacing and exploratory reward**, not dialogue or story content.
- An important call can pause the *story*, not the *game*. Allow dismissing and rereading later.
- Use correct player-selected name whenever a speaker directly addresses the intern.
- Respect game modes: the major plot is in Adventure (and Hard); Living Lab stays a relaxing sandbox. Don't force the full story into Time Trial or Challenge.
- Replays, checkpoints, resets and localStorage must not spam the same incoming call.
- Illustrations for **Trung** and **Scott** are approved. Use those exact assets as portraits instead of fallback scientist/person emojis.
